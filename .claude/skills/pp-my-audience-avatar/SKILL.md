# PP My Audience Avatar — "Dave"

Every topic, title, thumbnail, hook, script, and offer is written for ONE person: Dave.
Before drafting anything, re-read this profile and write directly to him. Never write
for "punters in general" — write for Dave specifically.

## Who Dave is

- Male, 35–60, Australia (psychology identical in UK/NZ). Employed — trades, small
  business, or office. Punting is his passion hobby that he wishes was a second income.
- The dangerous middle: 5–20 years of punting experience, still losing overall, and
  smart enough to know it. Not a first-time flutterer, not a pro.
- Watches the races Saturday, has 3–6 bookie apps, reads form on his lunch break.
- Genuinely loves racing, but carries quiet shame: after all these years he can't show
  a profit. Often keeps the true extent of his punting semi-private from his partner.
- At a crossroads: oscillating between "one more approach and I'll crack it" and
  "maybe I should give the game away."
- Secondary segment (younger Dave, 25–35): newer punter for whom the form guide is a
  wall of gibberish; he picks horses by name or gut and wants to stop feeling like he's
  guessing. Content for him = simplify and demystify.

## His biggest problems and frustrations (use his framing)

1. "I've tried everything and I still lose." He has attempted dozens of approaches —
   favourites, each-way value, form study, exotics, staking systems, Martingale — and at
   best made a small profit "not worth all the time." He doesn't lack information; he
   lacks a method he trusts.
2. "Tipsters are mostly liars." He has paid for services that hid losses, padded
   results, tipped odds-on rubbish, or vanished into excuses. He is deeply cynical but
   still searching. Transparency and reasoning are the trust currency, not strike rate.
3. "The form guide is gibberish" (younger segment). Overwhelmed by the numbers,
   letters, and abbreviations; wants to feel like he knows what he's doing.
4. "The game is rigged against the little guy." Odds crunch late, computer syndicates
   flood the pools, and in Australia the corporates restrict or ban you the moment you
   start winning while chasing "mug money" from losers.
5. "My own head is the enemy." He chases losses, blows a week of discipline in one
   tilt, bets for something to do, and knows it. The emotional swings affect his mood
   and family life.

## What he secretly wants (the real product)

1. Identity: to feel like a sharp, not a mug — the guy at the pub who understands the
   race. "Knowing what you're doing" is the core promise.
2. A repeatable process, not naked tips: rules, logic, staking, a reason for every bet,
   and the comfort of "even when I lose, I'm doing it right."
3. A respectable second income — or at minimum a hobby that pays for itself and
   finishes the year in front, with a couple of big collects to tell stories about.
4. Belonging: a clubhouse of like-minded punters (he half-dreams of a small syndicate
   of mates). Punting is lonely.
5. Validation: "you're not losing because you're dumb — you were never taught the
   right framework." This reframe is the doorway to every hook and every sale.

## What he already tried that failed (never pitch these as new ideas)

- Gut feel, horse names, pub tips → cringes at this era now.
- Multis / Lucky 15s / exotic combos → learned these are bookie traps; proud he knows.
- Backing favourites religiously → ground down; knows favourites lose ~3 of 4 races.
- DIY form study with no framework → hours of effort, paralysis by analysis, no edge.
- Paid tipsters (1–3 services) → burned; trust failed, not just profit.
- Martingale / staking tricks → blew a bank; knows staking can't fix bad selections.
- Chasing losses / doubling up → the classic wipeout; a psychology failure he admits.
- Free social-media tips → treats as entertainment, doesn't trust.

The gap: every failure was a search for winners. Nobody ever taught him value,
pricing, record-keeping, and discipline as one coherent system. That is what
Practical Punting sells.

crack the code · beat the game · find an edge · mug punter vs sharp · smart money vs

the getting-out stakes · "treat it as a hobby" (advice he's sick of) · in it for the

## How to apply this avatar to each content type

- Topics: pick subjects that hit one named pain or desire above. If a topic idea
  doesn't map to one, cut it or reframe it until it does.
- Titles/thumbnails: lead with his pain or identity in his own words. Patterns that
  fit: "Why you've tried 100 systems and still lose", "Stop punting like a mug",
  "The form guide, finally explained like a human", "What tipsters won't show you:
  the losing months", "Why the bookies ban winners (and want YOU)".
- Hooks (first 15 seconds): name the wound before the promise. Open with recognition
  ("You've done the form for an hour and still torn the ticket up...") so Dave thinks
  "that's me", then the reframe ("it's not you — it's the method you were handed").
- Scripts: teach the WHY behind every selection or concept. Show losing runs and full
  P&L openly — radical transparency is the #1 differentiator against tipsters he
  distrusts. Talk to him as an intelligent adult who's been burned, never down to him.
- Offers/CTAs: sell process, education, transparency, and community — never "get rich",
  never guaranteed winners, never urgency built on his losses.

## Guardrails (non-negotiable)

Part of this audience is close to problem gambling. Never exploit desperation,
chasing, secrecy, or "win it all back" emotions in any hook, title, or offer. Build in
discipline and bet-what-you-can-afford messaging as part of the brand — it is both
right and what the most-trusted services do. Avoid promises of guaranteed profit or
income replacement.
