# PP b-roll and hero imagery

> **The rule that ranks everything below it: PLAUSIBILITY BEATS BEAUTY.**
> Jodie rejected a hero photograph that had **horses running on both sides of the running rail.** It was a good-looking image and it is impossible. To a punter that is instantly disqualifying — it says we do not know the sport we are teaching. **A dull image costs a little. A wrong one costs credibility.**

---

## 1. Where generated imagery is allowed
- ✅ **B-roll clips** — footage under narration.
- ✅ **Hero photographs** — the cover, the thumbnail, the title card.
- ❌ **NEVER cards, covers-as-layout, figures or any typographic asset.** Those are authored HTML, rendered by the card system. Generated imagery never carries our words.

---

## 2. 🔴 THE PLAUSIBILITY CHECKLIST
**Run every generated image and clip against this BEFORE it is used. Any failure = discard and regenerate. Do not "use it anyway, nobody will notice." They will.**

**Racing correctness**
- [ ] **All runners on the SAME side of the running rail.** Always. No exceptions.
- [ ] **Direction of travel consistent** for every horse in frame.
- [ ] **Field size plausible** for the race implied, and consistent between shots of the same race.
- [ ] **Silks differ** between horses; no two runners in identical colours.
- [ ] **Gear and tack correct and complete** — saddle, bridle, number cloth present and sane.
- [ ] **Barrier stalls, running rail, winning post** correctly formed if visible.
- [ ] **Track surface and going match the claim** — a wet-track shot must actually look wet.

**Anatomy and physics**
- [ ] Four legs, one head, one rider per horse. No extra or merged limbs.
- [ ] No horses facing each other, overlapping impossibly, or floating.
- [ ] Rider seated plausibly, stirrups and reins attached to something real.

**Riders**
- [ ] **A realistic mix of riders, INCLUDING FEMALE JOCKEYS.** *(Jodie, 29 Jul 2026.)* **This is accuracy, not decoration** — Australian racing has a large and prominent female riding cohort, so a b-roll library of only male riders is the *less* realistic depiction, and this audience notices. Aim for a natural mix across the library, not one token image.
- [ ] **No identifiable real person.** Never generate a recognisable real jockey, trainer or owner, male or female. Riders are anonymous — helmet, goggles, distance, or angle.

**Brand and compliance**
- [ ] **NO bookmaker or wagering-operator branding, signage, logos or app screens.** Ever. *(Compliance, not taste — see the gambling-advertising rules in the SEO/compliance notes.)*
- [ ] No other real brand marks, sponsor boards or broadcaster furniture.

**Welfare optics — a racing channel cannot afford to look careless here**
- [ ] No falls, no fallen horses, no visible distress or injury.
- [ ] **No whip raised above shoulder height**; ideally no whip in shot at all.
- [ ] Horses look healthy, sound and well-handled.

**Context**
- [ ] **Australian** where the shot implies a place — Australian tracks, crowds, light and vegetation.
- [ ] **CONTEMPORARY.** Modern gear, modern crowds, modern light — see the ruling below.

---

## 2a. 🔒 THE B-ROLL IS MODERN. ALWAYS. (Jodie, 29 July 2026)

> **"I would prefer the b roll looks modern from now please. Put this as a rule."**

**B-roll and hero imagery depict CONTEMPORARY Australian racing on every episode,
whatever the source article's date.** The era-appropriate default is **RETIRED** — it
used to say *"a shot implying that period must not carry modern gear, modern signage
or modern crowds"*, and it is gone. **Do not restore it.**

**The reasoning, written down so nobody re-opens it:**

1. **Nothing in an episode claims to depict the year the article was written.** Gordon
   says when it was published and credits the author; the footage is illustration, not
   reconstruction. A 1992 dateline does not make a modern shot a false claim.
2. **One library serves all fifty episodes.** Our sources run from the 1980s and 90s,
   so an era rule would mean a separate period library per decade — clips that could
   never be reused, and a registry that fragments.
3. 🔑 **IT REMOVES A GENUINE COLLISION.** The era rule pulled directly against the
   female-jockey rule below: female riders were far rarer in Australian racing in 1992
   than now, so "period-accurate" and "realistic rider mix" could not both be
   satisfied on a historical episode. **The female-jockey rule now applies CLEANLY AND
   ALWAYS**, with nothing to trade it against.

*Caught on EP14 (July 1992), the first episode to test the era rule in anger. The
prompts were written era-accurate, the collision was flagged rather than quietly
resolved, and Jodie ruled. **Nothing had been generated, so the rewrite cost nothing;
ten minutes later it would have cost credits.***

---

## 3. What b-roll is FOR
**B-roll earns its place by showing what words cannot.** The run from the barrier to the first turn. A wet track. A wide draw. A field bunching. **B-roll that merely illustrates a noun is decoration**, and the coherence principle applies to footage exactly as it applies to cards: *removing filler improves comprehension.*

- **Never under a card.** While an on-screen card is visible the shot is WIDE and the b-roll is out. *(Standing rule — the EP11 card-over-the-face failure.)*
- **A b-roll clip starts when he starts describing the thing and ends before the next idea begins.** Placement is anchored to speech, never to a fixed slot.
- **If nothing visual is being described, no b-roll.** Silence on the picture track is allowed.

---

## 4. The house look
- Australian racing, natural light, real weather. Documentary rather than glossy.
- **Nothing that reads as stock-photo triumphalism** — no fists in the air, no confetti, no slow-motion glory.
- Consistent grade across a single episode; b-roll from one episode should look like it belongs to that episode.
- **Every hero and clip is logged in the b-roll registry** with a one-line description, so it can be found and never silently reused twice in a row.

---

## 5. Writing the prompt
1. **State the racing situation first**, in plain terms — *"a field of eight rounding the home turn, all on the inside of the running rail, wet track, overcast Australian afternoon."*
2. **Name what must be true** rather than what must not: *"all horses travelling the same direction, single running rail on the outside."* Negative prompts are less reliable than positive statements.
3. **Specify the rider mix** where riders are visible — **including female jockeys**.
4. ~~**Say the era** if it matters.~~ **RETIRED 29 July 2026 — see §2a.** The imagery is
   contemporary on every episode, so the era is never a prompt variable. If a prompt
   says a year, that is a bug.
5. **Generate more than you need** and discard against §2 — regenerating is cheaper than shipping a wrong image.
6. **Look at every one.** Do not accept an image you have not viewed. *(A machine that reports "generated 6 images" has told you nothing about whether any of them show racing.)*

---

## 6. Credits and cost
- **Higgsfield** for b-roll and hero photographs only. Watch the credit balance; log usage per episode.
- **Never Higgsfield for cards, figures or covers** — those are free to render and must carry exact words.

## 7. When something gets through
If a wrong image reaches Jodie, that is **not a one-off fix — it is a missing line in §2.** Add it, with the example, so it cannot happen twice.
